MEC homepage section ideas

What could sit below the new mission statement without turning the homepage into a generic club directory.

Ideation · 2026-09-10 · Homepage below the mission statement

Codebase context

The homepage currently establishes ambition through a headline, community photography, and a mission statement. The rest of the site already contains three useful rhythms: weekly Late Nights, a 36-hour Sprint, and the 10-week StartHouse program. Events and Get Involved provide clear destinations, while founder stories and outcome claims still need stronger verified material. The existing Apply route also needs repair before new application-heavy sections rely on it.

1. Three Speeds of Building

Show MEC through three memorable time scales: Every week for Late Night, 36 hours for Sprint, and 10 weeks for StartHouse. Each gets one sentence and one link.

Axis
How MEC works

Basis
Direct: These three rhythms already exist across the Events and Programs pages.

Why it matters
It makes the ambitious mission concrete without repeating the Programs grid. Visitors understand MEC as a progression from meeting people to building seriously.

Downside
The labels and program durations need to stay accurate.

Confidence 94%Complexity Low

2. Choose Your Next Move

Offer three intent-based choices: “I want to meet builders,” “I have an idea,” and “I’m already building.” Route each person to the most relevant event or program.

Axis
Visitor next step

Basis
Reasoned: New visitors understand their own situation before they understand MEC’s internal program names.

Why it matters
It turns inspiration into a personal action and reduces navigation friction.

Downside
Every choice must lead to a working destination. The current Apply route is not ready.

Confidence 90%Complexity Low

3. What Starting Looks Like

Feature three short, present-tense founder snapshots: testing an idea, shipping a first version, or finding a first customer. Use names and companies only when the details are verified.

Axis
Proof and credibility

Basis
Direct: The homepage has strong community photos, but current alumni and case-study material is still thin or placeholder-based.

Why it matters
Specific work in progress feels more credible than generic testimonials or vanity metrics.

Downside
Requires a reliable stream of real founder updates.

Confidence 82%Complexity Medium

4. MEC Right Now

Add one compact live strip with the next Late Night, current application status, and one recent founder milestone.

Axis
Freshness and maintenance

Basis
Direct: The Events page already carries recurring Late Night information, while relevant dates are otherwise spread across the site.

Why it matters
A single current signal makes the club feel active and gives the visitor something they can do now.

Downside
High maintenance risk unless the homepage and Events page share one data source.

Confidence 79%Complexity Medium

5. What MEC Helps You Get Unstuck On

Name four real founder problems: finding collaborators, testing an idea, shipping quickly, and meeting experienced founders. Pair each with one concrete MEC answer.

Axis
Visitor next step

Basis
Direct: Existing routes contain Late Nights, Sprint, StartHouse, mentors, and partners, but visitors must translate those offerings into personal value themselves.

Why it matters
It connects the mission statement to recognizable student friction.

Downside
The copy must stay concrete or it becomes generic benefits language.

Confidence 76%Complexity Low

6. Bring One Thing. Leave With Another.

Use a compact exchange format: “Bring an idea, leave with feedback,” “Bring a prototype, leave with sharper questions,” and “Come alone, meet other builders.”

Axis
How MEC works

Basis
Reasoned: Visitors are more likely to attend when they can picture what they contribute and receive.

Why it matters
It makes the community experience tangible without needing stats or long explanations.

Downside
Avoid guarantees such as promising everyone a cofounder or customer.

Confidence 72%Complexity Low
